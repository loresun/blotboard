Follow along once and in five minutes you'll have walked the main road: spread out → connect → tidy → read → take it with you.

## 1. Open a board

Top of the left sidebar, **New**. Click the board name in the middle of the top bar to rename it. Once you have a lot of boards you can group them: the little arrow next to the New button has "New group" and "New board inside a group".

## 2. Put something on it

Three ways to drop a card — use whichever is closest to hand:

- **Double-click empty canvas** — opens a text card right there, ready to type
- **Click a card kind in the toolbar** — lands in the center of the viewport and goes straight into edit mode
- **Drag / paste** — drag in an image, PDF, or audio/video file and it becomes a card; copy a URL and press `⌘V` for a link card

Don't agonise over which kind to use. **A text card is enough most of the time**, and if you want to switch later, right-click the card and choose "Change type".

## 3. Connect things

Each of a card's four sides has a small dot in the middle; drag from a dot onto another card to draw an edge. Edges aren't decoration — they carry one of five relations (related / blocks / precedes / references / produces) plus a strength from 1 to 5. Tidy, clustering and export all read them. Details in [Edges carry meaning](/docs?doc=edges).

## 4. Straighten it up

**Tidy**, on the right of the top bar. The first entry, "Neaten", is the everyday one: it **keeps the structure you laid out** and only aligns rows and columns, untangles crossing edges and pushes overlaps apart. To look at the same pile from a different angle, the same menu holds ten more layouts (unroll as flow, timeline, kanban, quadrants…), and every one of them can be undone. See [Eleven ways to tidy](/docs?doc=organize).

## 5. Read it once

**Reading** in the top bar (shortcut `R`): the whole board is flattened into one sequence in reading order, one card filling the screen, `←` `→` to turn. This is the fastest way to check whether the board actually says anything — however pretty the layout, if it doesn't hold together when read straight through, you haven't thought it through.

## 6. Take it with you

**Export**, top bar. The default entry, "Formatted export · HTML", gives you a **self-contained single file**: no external links, double-click to open offline, print it and you get an A4 PDF. There's also PNG (a screenshot of the whole board), Markdown (for humans or for feeding a model) and JSON (which can be loaded straight back in).
