**Templates** in the top bar. Pick one and the cards and their edges are laid out in one go; all you do is fill them in.

## The 13 built in

| Template | What it's for |
| --- | --- |
| Nine-box ideation | One center word, eight directions to branch out |
| SCAMPER | Substitute / combine / adapt… seven angles of transformation |
| First principles | Break it down until it can't be broken down, then build back up |
| 5 Whys | Ask why five times over to find the real cause |
| 5W1H question canvas | Turn a vague question into a clear one |
| Six thinking hats | Six perspectives, taken in turn |
| Golden circle: Why-How-What | Reason out from motive to method |
| SWOT analysis | Strengths and weaknesses × opportunities and threats |
| Cross-domain idea launcher | Borrow a solution from another field |
| Weekly review | What you did / what you learned / what changes next week |
| Topic kickoff skeleton | A topic from idea to ready-to-start |
| Book outline skeleton | Get the chapter structure down first |
| AI fit diagnosis | Decide whether something actually warrants AI |

## Two ways to use them

- **Open a new board**: pick a template and get a board already laid out
- **Insert into the current board**: the template's cards land near the current viewport and coexist with what's already there

## The "still to fill" hint

The cards a template lays out contain placeholder content. The template drawer tells you how many template cards are still unfilled — filled ones stop counting. That counter is there to stop you mistaking a half-finished board for a finished thought.

## Building your own templates

Tidy a board into the shape you want, export the JSON, and you have a reusable skeleton — import it next time and change the names. The source of truth for templates is the JSON files under `data/templates/` in the repo, in the same format as exported board JSON.
